MEMORANDUM

To: Kesar Industries Pvt. Ltd. (General Counsel)
From: Advisory Team
Re: Termination for Convenience of Manufacturing Supply Agreement with Anand Component Works Pvt. Ltd. - Cure Period and Damages Exposure
Date: 12 November 2021

This memorandum addresses whether Kesar Industries ("Kesar") may exercise its termination for convenience right under Clause 14.2 of the Manufacturing Supply Agreement dated 4 April 2019 with Anand Component Works Pvt. Ltd. ("Anand"), citing Anand's persistent shortfall against the minimum annual offtake commitment under Article 8 as its commercial reason for exiting, and what exposure Kesar faces if Anand asserts a damages claim.
## PAGE 2
1. BACKGROUND

Anand has fallen short of the 240,000-unit minimum annual offtake commitment for two consecutive years, most recently taking delivery of only 168,000 units in the twelve months to September 2021. Kesar issued a notice of termination for convenience under Clause 14.2 of the Agreement on 15 September 2021, citing the offtake shortfall as its reason and triggering the forty-five (45) day Cure Period, which expired on 30 October 2021 without the shortfall being addressed.

On 18 November 2021 - after the Cure Period had already expired - Anand's counsel wrote asserting that Kesar's own quality rejections had contributed to the shortfall and threatening a damages claim of approximately INR 3.2 crore should Kesar proceed to terminate.
## PAGE 3
2. ANALYSIS

2.1 Termination Mechanics. Clause 14.2 permits Kesar to terminate for convenience once the 45-day Cure Period tied to the identified concern has run without that concern being addressed. Kesar's notice was properly particularised and the Cure Period has fully expired. On its face, Kesar is entitled to terminate under Clause 14.2.

2.2 Timing of the Damages Claim. It is significant that Anand's damages assertion was raised only after the Cure Period had already expired, and not pre-emptively during the cure window. We considered Continental Constructions Co. Ltd v. State Trading Corporation of India, AIR 1997 Del 217, which holds that a cure period stipulated in a commercial contract must be permitted to run its full course before the innocent party may treat the underlying concern as one sounding in damages, and that an assertion of a damages claim prior to expiry of the cure period does not defeat the other party's opportunity to address that concern. Because Anand's claim was asserted only after the Cure Period had run - not before it, and not during it - this line of authority is not directly engaged in Kesar's favour or against it on the timing point; rather, it confirms that Kesar's own conduct (waiting out the full Cure Period before treating the exit as final) was procedurally correct and forecloses any argument that Kesar jumped the gun.

2.3 Recommendation. We advise that Kesar may proceed to issue a further notice finalising the termination under Clause 14.2. Anand's quality-rejection counter-narrative is a merits dispute properly ventilated in arbitration under Article 22, and does not affect the validity of the termination itself, which rests on Kesar's convenience right rather than on proving fault. Kesar's exposure to Anand's asserted INR 3.2 crore damages claim depends on the merits of the quality-rejection allegations, which we have not yet assessed in this memorandum.
